Healthy Weight for Your Height

Last updated: August 2026

There is no single correct weight for a height — there is a range, and it is wider than most people expect. At 170 cm (5 ft 7 in) the healthy band spans about 19 kg, or 42 pounds; opposite ends of it can both be perfectly healthy.

The tables below give the range for every height, from the standard BMI categories (healthy = 18.5 to 24.9) — then how to pick your own number inside it.

Why the healthy range is so wide

What varies between two people of identical height: muscle (denser than fat — a strong person legitimately weighs more), frame size (narrow to broad builds), sex (women carry more essential fat and often sit healthily lower in the band), age (for older adults the bottom of the range is often not the healthiest place — low weight in later life is linked to frailty), and ancestry (several health bodies set lower thresholds for South Asian and East Asian descent, so the top of these tables may be higher than your doctor advises).

It cuts both ways: inside the range is not proof of health; slightly outside it is not cause for alarm.

Finding your own number inside the range

Rather than aiming at a figure from a table:

  1. Check your waist — under half your height, a thirty-second check of the fat that matters most: waist-to-height ratio.
  2. Look where you have been stable — a weight held comfortably for a year without restricting is sustainable.
  3. Watch composition — training changes your body while weight stays flat; body fat percentage catches what the scale hides.
  4. Ask what your doctor watches — blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol. Weight is a proxy for those.
  5. Move slowly — a target within 5 to 10 percent of current weight is the one most people keep: how much weight you can safely lose per week.

Well outside the range? Start smaller: losing 5 percent already measurably improves blood pressure and blood sugar.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy weight for a 5 ft 6 in woman?

Roughly 115 to 154 lb, or 52 to 70 kg. The tables are not sex-specific, so women often sit healthily in the lower half of that band — but muscle, frame, and body composition all shift where an individual belongs within it.

Is there an ideal weight for my height?

Not a single number, no. Older formulas produced one figure per height, but they were built for medication dosing, not health, and ignore muscle entirely. A range is more honest — and where you sit inside it matters less than your waist, activity, and blood markers.

Should men and women use different weight-for-height tables?

The standard tables are the same for both, because they come straight from BMI, which does not distinguish by sex. For a sex-specific measure, body fat percentage has separate healthy ranges for men and women.

What if I'm above the range but very muscular?

Then the table is probably wrong about you. Weight scaled to height cannot tell muscle from fat, and trained people routinely exceed the healthy band while lean. Check your waist against half your height, and use the mirror and your strength as the real measures.

How long should it take to reach a healthy weight?

Longer than most plans suggest, and that is fine. About 0.5 to 1 kg (1 to 2 lb) per week is what most guidance considers sustainable — see safe weight loss rate. Reaching a 10 kg goal in five months and keeping it beats losing it in six weeks and regaining it.

Health disclaimer

This article is for general information only and is not medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Healthy weight ranges are population guidance and cannot account for your individual build, health conditions, or medications — always discuss weight targets and any plan to change your weight with your doctor or healthcare provider. Seek medical advice promptly if you are losing or gaining weight without trying, or if weight change comes with symptoms such as persistent fatigue, breathlessness, or loss of appetite.
