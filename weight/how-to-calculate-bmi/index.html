How to Calculate BMI

Last updated: August 2026

Body mass index is your weight divided by your height squared. One division, one multiplication — the whole reason the number has survived nearly two hundred years. You can do it on a phone keypad in ten seconds.

Both formulas are below, worked through with real numbers, plus the part most calculators skip: where the result misleads. Once you have your figure, the BMI chart tells you which category it falls into.

The metric formula: kilograms and meters

BMI = weight in kilograms ÷ (height in meters × height in meters)

The only trap is the unit: meters, not centimeters. 175 cm is 1.75 m — use 175 and you get a BMI of 0.0025.

Worked example

78 kg at 175 cm:

  1. Convert: 175 cm = 1.75 m
  2. Square: 1.75 × 1.75 = 3.0625
  3. Divide: 78 ÷ 3.0625 = 25.5

That is just inside overweight, which begins at 25.0 — about 1.5 kg from the healthy band. Never treat a result within a point of a boundary as firm: normal daily weight fluctuation can move you across the line and back within a week.

The imperial formula: pounds and inches

BMI = (weight in pounds × 703) ÷ (height in inches × height in inches)

The 703 converts pounds per square inch into kilograms per square meter — 39.37 inches per meter, squared, divided by 2.2046 pounds per kilogram — so both formulas give the same answer.

Worked example

172 lb at 5 ft 9 in:

  1. Height in inches: (5 × 12) + 9 = 69 in
  2. Square: 69 × 69 = 4,761
  3. Weight × 703: 172 × 703 = 120,916
  4. Divide: 120,916 ÷ 4,761 = 25.4

Same person as the metric example, same answer. The classic mistake: leaving feet and inches separate and squaring only the inches.

Where the number goes wrong

The arithmetic is simple, so wrong BMIs come from the inputs.

  • Guessed height. Most adults are a centimeter or two shorter than their license says, and height shrinks with age. At 175 cm, 1 cm moves BMI by about 0.3 — enough to cross a boundary. Measure barefoot against a wall, once, and reuse the figure.
  • Inconsistent weigh-ins. You can be 1 to 2 kg heavier in the evening. Weigh after waking, before eating.
  • Mixed units, squared weight, over-rounding. Kilograms with inches produce nonsense, and rounding height to the nearest 5 cm can shift the result a full point. Two calculators disagreeing is almost always one of these.

Then there is what the formula cannot see. Muscle is denser than fat, so a well-trained rower can post a BMI of 28 to 30 at single-digit body fat — if you lift seriously, treat BMI as close to meaningless and use body fat percentage instead. The reverse holds: someone sedentary at BMI 23 can carry enough abdominal fat to raise metabolic risk. Risk begins at different BMIs in different populations, the same BMI at 60 holds more fat than it did at 30, women carry more essential fat than men, BMI is not valid in pregnancy, and children use percentile charts. The cheapest fix is a tape measure: waist-to-height ratio takes thirty seconds and sees the fat distribution BMI cannot.

Frequently asked questions

What is the BMI formula?

In metric: weight in kg ÷ (height in m)². In imperial: (weight in lb × 703) ÷ (height in in)². Both give the same result — the 703 simply converts pounds and inches into the metric equivalent.

Why is 703 used in the imperial BMI formula?

It is the unit conversion baked into the equation: one meter is 39.37 inches, one kilogram is 2.2046 pounds, and 39.37² ÷ 2.2046 comes out at roughly 703.

Is BMI calculated differently for men and women?

No — the formula and the adult categories are identical, even though women carry more essential body fat at the same BMI. For a sex-specific measure, use body fat percentage, which has separate ranges.

How accurate is BMI for athletes?

Poor. Muscle weighs more than fat for the same volume, so trained athletes routinely land in the overweight or obese categories while being extremely lean. For anyone doing serious resistance training, waist measurement and body fat estimates are far more informative.

Do I need to recalculate BMI every time I weigh myself?

Not manually. Your height rarely changes, so BMI moves in lockstep with weight. Checking the figure monthly is plenty, and an app recalculates it automatically anyway.
