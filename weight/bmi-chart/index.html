BMI Chart for Adults

Last updated: August 2026

Body mass index compares your weight to your height and hands you one number. It is not precise — it is quick, free, and roughly predictive across large groups, which is why it is the first figure almost every clinician reaches for.

Below: the full adult chart, a height-and-weight lookup table, and what your category does and does not mean. Prefer the math? See how to calculate BMI.

The adult BMI chart

The World Health Organization thresholds for adults 20 and over, any sex, used essentially worldwide.

CategoryBMI rangeWhat it generally indicates
UnderweightBelow 18.5May signal undernutrition, illness, or a naturally light frame
Healthy weight18.5 – 24.9The range with the lowest average health risk
Overweight25.0 – 29.9Slightly raised average risk; body composition matters a lot here
Obesity class 130.0 – 34.9Raised risk of type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, joint problems
Obesity class 235.0 – 39.9Substantially raised risk; worth a conversation with a doctor
Obesity class 340.0 and aboveHighest risk category; medical support usually recommended

Two footnotes. The boundaries are conventions, not cliffs — 24.9 and 25.1 are the same body. And several health bodies set lower cutoffs for people of South Asian, Chinese, and other Asian ancestry (overweight from about 23, obesity from about 27.5), where diabetes and heart-disease risk rises earlier. Ask your doctor which set applies to you.

Each band is nearly 20 kg wide at 170 cm — there is no single correct weight for a height, which is why a healthy weight for your height is a target zone, not a target number.

What your category means — and what BMI can't see

A category is a starting point, not a verdict.

  • Underweight. Worth investigating, not celebrating — unintentional loss can point to thyroid problems, malabsorption, or an eating disorder, though some people are simply built light.
  • Healthy weight. Lowest average risk, no guarantee: little muscle plus abdominal fat at BMI 23 still carries metabolic risk.
  • Overweight. Where BMI is least informative — a muscular person and a sedentary one can share a BMI of 27. This is where waist-to-height ratio earns its keep.
  • Obesity classes 1 to 3. Misclassification gets rare as BMI rises; risk of type 2 diabetes, hypertension, sleep apnea, and joint pain climbs with each class.

The blind spots share one cause: BMI divides weight by height squared and nothing else. It cannot tell muscle from fat, cannot see where fat sits — fat around the organs is far more harmful than fat on the hips — and ignores age, sex, and ancestry. It is not valid in pregnancy, and children use percentile charts. Pair it with a tape measure and, for the fullest picture, body fat percentage.

In every category, direction beats the snapshot: a BMI drifting up half a point a year says more than any single reading.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good BMI for an adult?

Anything from 18.5 to 24.9 counts as a healthy weight, with no meaningful risk difference across the range. Aim to sit comfortably inside the band for your height rather than at a specific figure — see healthy weight for your height.

Is the BMI chart different for men and women?

No — the adult categories are identical, even though women naturally carry more body fat at the same BMI. That is a known limitation, and one reason body fat percentage uses separate ranges for men and women.

Does the BMI chart work for children?

No. Children and teenagers are assessed on age- and sex-specific percentile charts, because healthy body composition changes throughout growth — a child's BMI is compared to other children the same age, not to the adult thresholds above.

Can you be healthy with a BMI over 25?

Yes, and many people are — particularly if the extra weight is muscle, your waist is under half your height, and your blood pressure, blood sugar, and cholesterol are normal. BMI is a screening tool; those other markers are what your doctor acts on.

How often should I check my BMI?

Monthly is plenty. BMI only moves when weight moves meaningfully — checking daily just exposes you to normal day-to-day fluctuation that has nothing to do with body composition.

Health disclaimer

This article is for general information only and is not medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. BMI is a screening tool and cannot assess an individual's health on its own — always discuss your weight, and any plan to change it, with your doctor or healthcare provider. Seek medical advice promptly if you are losing or gaining weight without trying, or if weight change comes with symptoms such as persistent fatigue, breathlessness, or loss of appetite.
